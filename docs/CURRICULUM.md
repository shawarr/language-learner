# The curriculum, A1.1 to C2.2

What the course teaches, in what order, and why that order. Measured against the standards real
German courses are built on, so that "finished B1.2 here" means roughly what it means at a
Goethe-Institut or in a German integration course.

### B2 (B2.1, B2.2): new
Zustandspassiv and the Passiv substitutes (sich lassen, sein + zu, -bar); genitive prepositions
aufgrund, infolge, hinsichtlich; kausal, konsekutiv, konzessiv and adversativ connectors (da,
zumal, sodass, folglich, obgleich, dennoch, wohingegen); Nomen-Verb-Verbindungen; Futur I for
assumptions and Futur II; Konjunktiv II of the past with modal verbs (hätte … müssen) and its word
order in subordinate clauses; als ob; relative clauses with wer … der; Konjunktiv I and indirect
speech; Redewiedergabe (laut, zufolge); subjective sollen and wollen; nominalisation; extended
participial attributes; modal clauses indem, dadurch dass, ohne dass; word formation; text
references; Nominal- and Verbalstil; es as placeholder and correlate; conditionals without wenn;
formal prepositions gemäß, mangels; the Gerundivum; describing a graph; participle clauses.

### C1 (C1.1, C1.2): new
Systematic nominalisation ↔ verbalisation; Konjunktiv I complete (questions, requests: er solle);
hedging (scheinen zu, gelten als, es ist davon auszugehen); all Passiv substitutes and Partizip
attributes ↔ relative clauses; subjective modal verbs in the past (muss gewesen sein); Vorfeld and
Thema–Rhema; advanced connectors (insofern … als, es sei denn, geschweige denn, ungeachtet);
genitive prepositions of the written register (zugunsten, seitens, mittels); concession (wenngleich,
so … auch, mag … auch); zu …, als dass; Funktionsverbgefüge with passive meaning; es-constructions;
Ausklammerung and the Nachfeld in speech; Konjunktiv II of the old strong forms (stünde, hülfe);
erlebte Rede; future in the past (sollte … werden); diplomatic indirectness.

### C2 (C2.1, C2.2): new
The Goethe C2 has no grammar list: the target is range and control. Idiom and phraseology; register
shifting (Amtsdeutsch ↔ plain German ↔ colloquial speech, including spoken grammar like weil + verb
second); the national standard varieties of Austria and Switzerland; irony, understatement and
implicature; Sprachmittlung (summarising across languages); essay and commentary style and
rhetorical figures; specialist discourse (standards, expert reports); legal and administrative
German; literary German; speeches.

## 3. Skills

The four exam skills, and where each is trained:

- **Sprechen**: Talk (scenarios per unit), the "say it" steps of every lesson, the spoken checkpoint.
- **Schreiben**: Write (a task per unit), the written checkpoint.
- **Hören**: every lesson has at least one `text` step with `skill: "listen"` (a dialogue or an
  announcement, heard before it is read), plus the listen-and-choose practice on single items.
- **Lesen**: every lesson from A1.2 on has a `text` step with `skill: "read"` (a message, an ad, a
  letter, an article), followed by checks on its content.

## 4. The syllabus

A1.1–B1.2 keep their units, with the moves and additions of §2. The
new phases:

### B2.1 — Making the case
1. **b2.1-1 Anerkennung und Behörden**: having a foreign degree recognised, residence after the Blue
   Card. Grammar: Passiv in all tenses incl. subordinate clauses with modal verbs; Zustandspassiv;
   Passiversatz with sich lassen, sein + zu, -bar; genitive prepositions aufgrund, infolge,
   hinsichtlich, bezüglich, anstelle. Scenarios: calling the Anerkennungsstelle; advice at the
   IQ-Beratung; explaining the process to a friend. Student: uni-assist and the Zulassung,
   recognition of credits.
2. **b2.1-2 Arbeit der Zukunft**: AI, automation, the four-day week. Grammar: kausal da, zumal;
   konsekutiv sodass, so … dass, folglich; konzessiv obgleich, dennoch, nichtsdestotrotz; adversativ
   während, wohingegen; Nomen-Verb-Verbindungen I (eine Entscheidung treffen, in Frage stellen);
   Futur I for assumptions and Futur II; infinitive in the past (behaupten, … zu haben).
3. **b2.1-3 Versicherungen und Gesundheit**: Haftpflicht, damage claims, the Krankenkasse refusing a
   cost. Grammar: Konjunktiv II of the past with modal verbs and in subordinate clauses (…, dass ich
   es hätte melden müssen); als ob / als wenn; relative clauses with wer … der and with dessen /
   deren; verbs, nouns and adjectives with fixed prepositions.
4. **b2.1-4 Medien und Information**: news, fake news, data protection, the Rundfunkbeitrag.
   Grammar: Konjunktiv I and indirect speech (er sei, habe; with Konjunktiv II where forms
   coincide; the past: er habe gesagt); Redewiedergabe with laut, zufolge, nach Angaben; subjective
   sollen and wollen; nominalisation of verbs.
5. **b2.1-5 Stadt und Umwelt**: waste separation and the Hausordnung, the city's climate plans,
   cars in the city. Grammar: extended participial attributes (die von der Stadt geplante
   Fahrradstraße); modal clauses with indem, dadurch dass, ohne dass, (an)statt dass; word
   formation (compounds, -ung, -heit, -keit, -schaft, -bar, -los); text references (dies, dabei,
   ersteres / letzteres).

### B2.2 — Professional German
1. **b2.2-1 Bewerbung und Arbeitszeugnis**: CV, cover letter, the coded language of a German
   reference letter. Grammar: Nominalstil ↔ Verbalstil; Nomen-Verb-Verbindungen II (in Betracht
   ziehen, Verantwortung tragen); adjective endings complete (after viele, einige, mehrere; no
   article); sowohl … als auch and je … umso in argument.
2. **b2.2-2 Besprechungen leiten**: chairing, minutes, decisions. Student: the group project
   meeting, the Fachschaft. Grammar: modal particles halt, eben, wohl, ruhig, nun mal; Konjunktiv II
   for diplomatic proposals (Wäre es denkbar, dass …); minutes in Konjunktiv I and Passiv (Es wurde
   beschlossen, dass …); es as placeholder and correlate (Es freut mich, dass …).
3. **b2.2-3 Verträge und Rechte**: the employment contract, notice, the Betriebsrat, tenancy law.
   Student: the Werkstudent contract. Grammar: conditionals without wenn (Sollte …, …); concession
   with so … auch, wie … auch, was … auch immer; formal prepositions gemäß, entsprechend, mangels,
   zuzüglich; the Gerundivum (die zu zahlende Miete) and sein + zu.
4. **b2.2-4 Technik verständlich erklären**: explaining a technical process to non-experts;
   describing a graph. Grammar: process descriptions with Passiv and substitutes; consecutive zu …,
   als dass; definitions and paraphrase (Unter X versteht man …); describing a graph (steigen um /
   auf, der Anteil, im Vergleich zu).
5. **b2.2-5 Gesellschaft und Politik**: elections, the federal system, Vereine and the Ehrenamt.
   Grammar: Präteritum in written history; relative clauses with wo(r)- (worüber, womit);
   temporal seitdem, solange, ehe, kaum dass; participle clauses (In Berlin angekommen, …).

### C1.1 — Precision
1. **c1.1-1 Sprache und Identität**: multilingualism, Heimat, belonging. Grammar: nominalisation ↔
   verbalisation, systematically: temporal (bei, nach, vor, während + noun ↔ als, nachdem, bevor),
   causal and concessive (wegen, trotz ↔ weil, obwohl), modal (durch ↔ indem), final (zur ↔ um … zu).
2. **c1.1-2 Wissenschaftlich und fachlich schreiben**: the design document / RFC (engineer), the
   Seminararbeit (student). Grammar: Konjunktiv I complete (questions, requests: er solle);
   hedging (scheinen zu, gelten als, es ist davon auszugehen, dass); all Passiv substitutes incl.
   bekommen-Passiv; participial attributes ↔ relative clauses.
3. **c1.1-3 Führung und Verantwortung**: delegating, the difficult appraisal, saying no upward.
   Student: leading a lab group as a Hiwi. Grammar: subjective modal verbs in present and past (Er
   muss es vergessen haben; Das dürfte schwierig werden); modal particles for tact; Konjunktiv II of
   the past for regret and criticism; Vorfeld and Thema–Rhema.
4. **c1.1-4 Wirtschaft und Konsum**: inflation, sustainable consumption, a company's numbers.
   Grammar: insofern … als, es sei denn, vorausgesetzt, geschweige denn, ungeachtet; zugunsten,
   zulasten, seitens, mittels, kraft; je nachdem; statistics at full precision.
5. **c1.1-5 Konflikte lösen**: mediation, a neighbourhood dispute, a Widerspruch. Grammar: wenngleich,
   so sehr … auch, mag … auch; zu …, als dass with Konjunktiv II; Funktionsverbgefüge with passive
   meaning (zur Sprache kommen, in Kraft treten); es geht um / es handelt sich um / es kommt darauf an.

### C1.2 — Full range
1. **c1.2-1 Vortrag und Referat**: the conference talk (engineer), the Referat (student). Grammar:
   Ausklammerung and the Nachfeld in speech; signposting (Wie eingangs erwähnt …); cohesion with
   pronominal adverbs across a talk; when nominal style helps and when it hurts in speech.
2. **c1.2-2 Debatte**: the ethics of AI, Streitkultur, the talk show. Grammar: zwar … aber,
   allerdings, hingegen, demgegenüber; concede-and-refute (Es mag sein, dass …, doch …); Konjunktiv II
   for hypothetical argument; modal verb substitutes (ist gezwungen, ist imstande, es ist erforderlich).
3. **c1.2-3 Literatur und Film**: reading a novel, writing a review. Grammar: literary Präteritum and
   Plusquamperfekt; the old Konjunktiv II forms (stünde, käme, gäbe, wüsste); erlebte Rede;
   participial constructions in literature.
4. **c1.2-4 Geschichte und Erinnerung**: 1989, the Gastarbeiter, the culture of remembrance. Grammar:
   Vorgangs- and Zustandspassiv in historical texts; kaum dass, ehe; future in the past (Er sollte
   nie zurückkehren); genitive chains.
5. **c1.2-5 Verhandeln auf hohem Niveau**: negotiating with a client, crisis communication. Student:
   negotiating an internship contract. Grammar: diplomatic Konjunktiv; indirectness; prepositional
   correlates (darauf bestehen, dass; davon ausgehen, dass); the middle field in detail (pronouns
   first, where nicht goes).

### C2.1 — Nuance and style
1. **c2.1-1 Redewendungen**: idioms and proverbs (nicht alle Tassen im Schrank, auf dem Holzweg sein),
   collocations, when an idiom is too much.
2. **c2.1-2 Register wechseln**: Amtsdeutsch into plain German and back; spoken grammar (weil + verb
   second, Ellipsen, tun-periphrasis); youth language; the long nested participial attribute of
   official texts.
3. **c2.1-3 Deutschland, Österreich, Schweiz**: the three standard varieties (Jänner, Matura, Velo,
   grüezi), the southern Perfekt (ich bin gesessen), understanding dialect.
4. **c2.1-4 Ironie und Zwischentöne**: irony, understatement, litotes (nicht ganz unproblematisch),
   rhetorical questions, what is meant but not said.
5. **c2.1-5 Sprachmittlung**: conveying a German letter to family in Arabic or English terms, and an
   English or Arabic text into German; summarising for a purpose; interpreting at an appointment.

### C2.2 — Mastery
1. **c2.2-1 Essay und Kommentar**: the essay, the commentary, the Glosse; rhetorical figures,
   sentence rhythm, varied sentence openings.
2. **c2.2-2 Fachdiskurs**: expert-level discourse: standards (DIN), expert reports, peer review;
   specialist word formation.
3. **c2.2-3 Recht und Verwaltung**: reading a law and a Bescheid, a reasoned Widerspruch; legal
   German (Wer …, wird …; sofern nicht; unbeschadet).
4. **c2.2-4 Literatur in der Tiefe**: Kafka, Brecht, Bachmann, contemporary writers; archaic and
   literary forms, word order for emphasis.
5. **c2.2-5 Ein Leben auf Deutsch**: the capstone: a speech (a wedding, a farewell, a thank-you),
   the naturalisation interview, one's own story at full range.

## 5. Authoring rules

For every unit, in `app/curriculum/lessons.json`:

- **10 to 16 steps**, roughly 12–20 minutes. Every grammar point in the unit's `grammar` list has its
  own `grammar` step (English explanation, a table where forms matter, 3–5 examples) and at least one
  `check` after it. Every vocabulary theme has a `words` step of 6–10 items.
- **At least one `text` step** (`skill: "listen"` from A1.1, `skill: "read"` from A1.2 on; both from
  A2.1 on), each followed by two `check`s on its content. Texts are realistic: an announcement, a
  voicemail, a flat ad, an email, an article, a dialogue in the unit's own situations. Length: 4–6
  lines at A1, 6–10 at A2, 8–14 at B1, 10–16 at B2 and above (a line is one or two sentences).
- **Explanations are in English at every level**, plain and short: the learner is reading on a phone.
  From B2 the examples and texts carry the weight; from C1 the `body` may quote German terms freely.
- Checks have three options, one right, a `why` that teaches. Wrong options are plausible mistakes,
  never jokes.
- German is correct, current and natural (what a German colleague would actually say), with the
  learner's world in it: placeholders `{{name}}`, `{{role_de}}`, `{{city}}`, `{{he}}`, … never a
  real name. Content about one learner's work carries a `variants.student` (or `variants.engineer`)
  block with the other version.
- Words and sentences meant to be spoken stay short (a `words` item of more than 8 words is not
  practised aloud).

For every scenario in `units.json`: `brief` (two or three English sentences: where you are, who you
are talking to, what you want) and 6–8 `words` (the phrases the learner will actually need, at the
unit's level), in each variant too.

## 6. How the content was built, and how to change it

All twelve phases are in `units.json` and every unit has a lesson in `lessons.json`. Two tests hold
the line: `test_every_unit_from_a1_to_c2_is_taught` in `tests/test_lessons.py`
and `test_every_scenario_comes_with_a_briefing_and_its_words`. A scene without a
student variant is played by both learners, so its setup never casts the other person as a work
colleague, and setups name third parties instead of using bare pronouns (the persona tests check
this, since a bare "he" is how a learner gets misgendered).

Facts that date (fees, deadlines, residence rules) are kept general or marked as examples; the ones
worth re-checking now and then are in b2.1-1 (recognition fees), b2.2-3 (labour and tenancy law),
c2.2-3 (Bescheid and Widerspruch) and c2.2-5 (naturalisation).
